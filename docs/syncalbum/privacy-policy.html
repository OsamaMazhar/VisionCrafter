Privacy Policy

SyncAlbum by Orbitaar Solutions · Effective 15 November 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what personal data SyncAlbum uses on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android, why, and what your rights are. It applies wherever you live. It is written to meet the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR); extra information for some countries is at the end.

The Short Version

  • Your photos, videos and comments are never sent to us. They live in your own iCloud (and, if you choose, your own Google Drive).
  • There is no SyncAlbum account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. We do not sell personal data.
  • To make sharing work, a small set of sharing records (your display name, account identifiers, join requests and activity notices) is kept in an iCloud database that we manage. Other iCloud users can read these records. From SyncAlbum 5.1 you can delete them yourself in the app, and the app removes old ones automatically. Details are below.
  • The companies involved are Apple, Google and Cloudflare, plus GitHub for the website.

We are responsible for the sharing records, the Android notification service and support email described below. The content of your albums sits in your own iCloud or Google Drive, where we cannot see it; Apple and Google store it under their agreements with you.

Where Your Albums Live

Albums, photos, videos, captions, comments, reactions, favourites and journal entries are stored in Apple's iCloud under your Apple Account, on every platform. SyncAlbum for Android also keeps albums in iCloud, so it needs an Apple Account too. Apple encrypts this data in transit and on its servers; how Apple protects it, including the option of end-to-end encryption with Advanced Data Protection, is described in Apple's iCloud security overview.

When you share an album, the people you approve can see and add to it through iCloud sharing. Their contributions are stored in your iCloud, and the albums you join are stored in their owner's iCloud.

Google Drive (optional). You can keep an album's full-resolution files in your own Google Drive instead of iCloud. If you do, you sign in with Google, and SyncAlbum can access only the files it creates, never the rest of your Drive. The files are encrypted on your device before upload, with a key Google doesn't have, so Google sees only file sizes and dates. Your Google account's name and email are shown in the app and are not sent to us.

Private and hidden albums. Private albums are stored on your device only and never sync. Hidden albums can be locked with Face ID, Touch ID, or your Android fingerprint or face unlock. The check is done by the operating system, and SyncAlbum never sees your biometric data.

Sharing and Joining

An album is shared only when you share its link or QR code. Everyone who opens the link in SyncAlbum asks to join, and gets in only after the owner approves. SyncAlbum alerts the owner if someone is in one of their albums without approval, and the owner can remove anyone at any time. Your display name and the photos, comments and reactions you add are visible to everyone in that album.

Albums shared before SyncAlbum 5.0 use an older iCloud link. SyncAlbum still asks for the owner's approval, but the older link itself isn't locked by iCloud, so someone who has it and uses it outside SyncAlbum could see and add to the album until the owner removes them. Making the album private (album menu → Make Private) locks the link, and everyone already in the album stays in.

People in an album may save copies of photos, for example to their own library or as a screenshot, while they have access. Copies they saved stay with them after access is removed, and we cannot control them.

Photos often show other people. Please add photos, names and comments about others only if you are allowed to.

Sharing Records We Process

To let people find each other and join albums, the app writes a few records to an iCloud database that Apple hosts for SyncAlbum. We manage this database and can see these records. Other iCloud users, not only the people in your albums, can read them:

  • Your display name and a pseudonymous Apple Account identifier. The identifier is a code Apple creates for SyncAlbum. It is not your Apple Account email or phone number, but it stays the same and is linked to your display name. Your display name is published when you share an album, join one or ask to join one, so the album owner and members can see who you are.
  • Join requests and answers: the identifiers of the person asking and of the album owner, an album identifier, the status (pending, approved, left) and dates.
  • Membership changes: notices that an album was deleted or someone's access was removed, or an album was made private, with identifiers and dates.
  • Activity notices that tell members something changed, such as a new photo or comment: the identifiers of the person who did it and of the person to tell, an album identifier, the type of activity and a photo identifier. They never contain the photo or the comment itself.
  • Invites and album keys, stored only as encrypted or signed data.

These records never contain photos. Since SyncAlbum 5.0, join requests show a neutral placeholder instead of the album's name, and their details are encrypted so only the album owner can read them.

You need a display name and an Apple Account signed in to iCloud to share or join albums. Without them, sharing does not work.

Other Data We Process

Android notifications. A small notification service run by us and hosted by Cloudflare tells Android phones when an album changes. It receives random codes that stand for albums and the sending phone's network (IP) address. The address is held in memory for about a minute to limit abuse and is then gone; nothing is stored and only counts are logged. The service then asks Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging to wake the other phones. That message has no content: no photos, names or text. Firebase uses a notification token for the app on each phone.

On-device features on Android. SyncAlbum for Android uses Google's ML Kit to recognise what is in a photo, find faces for framing, read text, cut out subjects, write summaries and scan QR codes. This happens on your device and your photos are not sent to Google. ML Kit itself sends Google usage and performance statistics: device model and system version, app version, an installation identifier, timings and error codes. From SyncAlbum 5.1, the app asks before it first uses ML Kit, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings › Privacy (Google ML Kit Features). If you say no, these features are off and nothing is sent.

Place names. If you allow location access, the coordinates of a photo are sent to Apple (iPhone, iPad, Mac) or Google (Android) to look up the place name.

Faces. Face-aware framing only finds where a face is in a picture, on your device. SyncAlbum does not recognise or identify people and stores no face prints or other biometric identifiers.

Stories and journal. Video stories, the journal's days, places and chapters, and voice-note transcripts are made on your device. Nothing is sent to us.

Diagnostics. SyncAlbum keeps a diagnostic log on your device. It leaves the device only if you choose to send it to us. SyncAlbum has no crash reporting or analytics of its own.

Support. If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you choose to send.

Purchases. Unlimited Albums and earlier purchases are handled by Apple or Google Play. We receive no payment details and do not see who bought. The app may show the store's standard rating prompt, which the store handles.

Website. orbitaar.com and its invite pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security, under its own privacy policy. The website uses no cookies, analytics or trackers. In SyncAlbum invite links, the secret part after "#" is never sent to the website.

Service notices. From SyncAlbum 5.1, the app checks our website about once a day for a short, signed notice, so that we can reach every user if we ever have to, for example about a security issue. The request carries no identifiers, cookies or parameters, but GitHub, which hosts the website, sees your IP address as with any website visit. A notice is shown once, in the app. A notice can also require an update before an older version uploads again.

Sharing records. From SyncAlbum 5.1, the app removes the sharing records you created when they are no longer needed. It checks about once a week, when you open it:

  • activity notices: after 30 days;
  • join requests still waiting for an answer: after 90 days;
  • declined, expired or left join requests and their answers: 30 days after the last change;
  • notices that an album was deleted or someone's access was removed: a week after the person has seen it, and at the latest after 12 months;
  • your display name, approved requests, blocks and invite links: kept because the albums you share or belong to need them. They are not removed automatically when you stop sharing or a request is declined; delete your sharing data to remove them.

This only happens when you open SyncAlbum 5.1 or later. If you stop using SyncAlbum, or keep an older version, your records stay until you delete them or email us. We delete them within 30 days of your email. Removing records you still need can stop a shared album from working for you.

We do not use your data for advertising, profiling or automated decisions, and we do not sell it.

Where these companies act for us, they may use the data only for that purpose. Where they act for themselves (for example your iCloud or Google account, purchases and ML Kit statistics), their own privacy policies apply. We may also have to disclose data if the law requires it. We share data with no one else.

Transfers Outside Your Country

These companies process data in the United States and in other countries where they have data centres. For data from the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, such transfers rely on an adequacy decision (for the United States, the Data Privacy Framework, where the company is certified) or on standard contractual clauses. Because album members can be anywhere, the sharing records can also be read by SyncAlbum users in any country; this is needed to provide shared albums (GDPR Art. 49(1)(b)). Email us for more information or a copy of the safeguards.

Permissions and Data on Your Device

SyncAlbum asks for access to your photos and videos, camera, location, microphone and speech recognition, biometric unlock and notifications only to provide the related features: importing photos, the built-in camera, place names, voice notes, locking hidden albums and alerts about album activity. You can change any permission in the device's Settings at any time. SyncAlbum works without the ones you don't grant.

The app stores your albums, settings, encryption keys and notification token on your device because the features you use need them. The only information read from your device that is not needed for a feature is the ML Kit statistics on Android described above, which are sent only with your consent.

Your Rights

You can ask us to:

  • tell you what personal data we hold about you and give you a copy;
  • correct data that is wrong (you can change your display name in the app);
  • delete your data;
  • restrict the processing of your data;
  • give you your data in a common, machine-readable format.

You can object at any time to processing that we base on our legitimate interests (see the table above). Where processing is based on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time: location in your device's Settings, ML Kit in SyncAlbum's Settings › Privacy (Google ML Kit Features).

Most of your data is in your own iCloud or Google Drive, where you can view, export and delete it yourself. You can delete your sharing records in the app (see "Deleting Your Data"). For everything else, email us. It costs nothing. We reply within one month; the law allows two more months for complex requests, and we will tell you if we need them. Because there is no SyncAlbum account, we may ask for details that let us find your records and check that the request comes from you.

You can complain to the Dutch Data Protection Authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl), or to the authority where you live or work.

Deleting Your Data

Delete photos or albums in the app, or leave albums you joined. Deleting an album you own removes it for everyone in it. Signing out on a device removes that account's albums from it; albums you own stay in your iCloud. You can also manage SyncAlbum's iCloud data in your Apple Account settings (iCloud), and its Google Drive files in Google Drive. Deleting the app removes what it stored on the device, including private albums.

Deleting the app does not remove your sharing records. From SyncAlbum 5.1 you can remove them yourself in Settings › Privacy › Delete Sharing Data. You can choose to:

  • delete your sharing data: you leave every album you joined, your photos, videos, comments and likes in those albums are deleted for everyone, and your requests and notices are removed. Albums you share stay shared, so your display name, your answers to join requests and your invite links stay, because the people in those albums need them;
  • stop sharing all and delete everything: the same, and every album you own stops being shared. Your albums stay on your devices, private, and your display name and invite links are removed too.

When you leave an album, a short note that you left stays for about 30 days, so the owner's app can remove you from the album. After that it is deleted. If you delete the app before then, email us to remove it. Albums you removed with "Remove for Now" in SyncAlbum 5.0 are included: your photos, comments, likes and journal entries in them are deleted too. If an album can no longer be reached, the app tells you, and you can ask the album's owner or us to remove what is left.

You can also email contact(at)orbitaar(dot)com with the subject "Delete my data", from any version. Settings › Privacy shows your SyncAlbum ID; include it so we can find your records. If you use the Email us button there, the app also saves a short code with your SyncAlbum ID for 30 days, so we can check the email really comes from you. We delete your records within 30 days.

Security

Data travels encrypted between your device, Apple, Google and Cloudflare. Google Drive files are encrypted on your device before upload. New shared albums are private: only people the owner approved can open them. Hidden albums can be locked with your device's biometric unlock. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach puts you at high risk, we will tell you as the law requires.

Children

SyncAlbum is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. If you are under 16, or under the age your country sets for agreeing to online services, use SyncAlbum only with a parent's or guardian's permission. Parents who believe their child has used SyncAlbum without permission can email us, and we will delete the child's sharing records.

United States, Including California

  • What we collect: identifiers (display name, pseudonymous account identifiers, IP address at the notification service, email address if you write to us) and app activity in shared albums (join requests and activity notices). We collect no sensitive personal information and no biometric identifiers.
  • Source and purpose: from you and from other members of your albums, only to run shared albums, deliver notifications and give support.
  • Disclosed to: the service providers listed under "Other Companies Involved", and other iCloud users as described under "Sharing Records We Process".
  • Kept: as described under "Why We Process It and How Long We Keep It".

Whichever state you live in, you can ask us to tell you what we hold, correct it or delete it, by email. You can use an authorised agent. We will not treat you differently for asking, and if we refuse a request you can ask us to reconsider by replying to our answer. Because SyncAlbum and our website do not track you, they behave the same whether or not your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal.
